6. **MEDIUM — The phone holds a second reorder queue for software decoders that already output in presentation order.** For H.264, FFmpeg buffers delayed pictures internally and selects the next output by picture order count. A-2 then puts those outputs into another queue sized from the stream’s reorder depth. A software-decoded stream with B-frames pays that delay and memory cost twice. It can exceed the phone’s admission budget even when FFmpeg’s ordered output would play with a small presentation lead. Size the external queue from the selected decoder’s **output order**, and account for decoder-internal pictures separately. 7. **MEDIUM — Display misses can trigger an unauthorized Automatic quality step-down.** A-2 adds the renderer’s dropped-frame count to late decodes. Apple’s count includes frames that missed their **display** deadline. With Automatic selected, sustained presentation delays can therefore cross Vela’s decoder-drop threshold even when decoding kept up and the network cache was healthy. The observable result is an interrupted play and possible loss of HDR. Feed the detector only drops attributable to decoding. Keep renderer misses as separate playback diagnostics. 8. **MEDIUM — A midstream format change can strand a working VideoToolbox session.** The plan negotiates the session and software fallback at startup. An HLS discontinuity or an Annex B stream can introduce new parameter sets and dimensions after minutes of successful playback. Apple explicitly allows a session to reject the new format description. Continuing with the initial description or session can freeze the picture while audio advances. On a format change, construct the new description, ask whether the session accepts it, and recreate or reroute the decoder at a random-access point while preserving the play clock. 10. **MEDIUM — “Exact” seek skips the picture and audio covering the requested time.** The engine discards every picture whose presentation timestamp precedes the target and restarts the clock at the next picture. If a variable-frame-rate picture spans 10–20 seconds, seeking to 12 seconds starts at 20. A seek inside the final picture may find no later picture at all. P2 measured a silent HEVC clip with the next picture within one frame of each target. Start the clock at the requested time, retain the picture whose presentation interval contains it, and trim audio across that boundary. 11. **MEDIUM — Counting each E-AC-3 syncframe advances the audio clock twice for dependent substreams.** An independent syncframe and its dependent syncframe can carry channels for the **same** time interval. Dolby gives a concrete example with two substreams and four three-block frames in one 1,536-sample access unit. Applying A-2’s one-frame-per-buffer rule advances the clock by 3,072 samples. That produces drift or repeated discontinuity flushes and can separate the dependent channels from their audio. Preserve complete access units and advance time once per decoded sample interval across all associated substreams. [Compressed-audio timing](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:493) · [One-frame rule](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:503) · [Dolby’s access-unit examples](https://ott.dolby.com/OnDelKits/DDP/Dolby_Digital_Plus_Online_Delivery_Kit_v1.5/Documentation/Playback/SDM/help_files/topics/c_dash_sample.html)

12. **MEDIUM — The HLS length rule rejects a valid read spanning adjacent segments.** FFmpeg 9 extends a single I/O open across consecutive byte ranges in the same resource. For two adjacent 1 KiB segments it can request 2 KiB. Applying the plan’s exact single-segment response-length check rejects a valid response or truncates it before the second segment. Validate the HTTP response against FFmpeg’s requested `offset` and `end_offset`, and enforce each logical segment’s boundary when consuming it. [Transport rule](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:343) · [FFmpeg’s range-coalescing implementation](https://ffmpeg.org/doxygen/9.0/hls_8c_source.html#l01481)

13. **MEDIUM — The range reader cannot guarantee one representation with a weak or absent validator.** The plan reuses the first ETag or Last-Modified value in `If-Range`. Use only qualifying validators. Otherwise retain one response or take a stable local snapshot, and restart the logical open before delivering bytes from a replacement.

## New findings

1. **HIGH — T0 cannot pass the repository’s Rust build.** T0 prescribes Tauri 2.12.7 on every desktop and requires the full verification set to pass. That release declares `rust-version = "1.90"`. Vela declares 1.89, and CI explicitly runs `cargo +1.89.0 check --locked`. Cargo will reject the upgraded dependency before compiling Vela. Make the compiler-floor change part of T0, updating the manifest, CI and verification guidance together. If the 1.89 floor must be retained, T0 needs a dependency set compatible with it. [Plan line 1063](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1063) · [Vela manifest](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/src-tauri/Cargo.toml:10) · [CI command](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.github/workflows/ci.yml:44) · [Tauri 2.12.7 manifest](https://github.com/tauri-apps/tauri/blob/tauri-v2.12.7/Cargo.toml#L36-L46)

2. **HIGH — The software fallback loses profile 5 Dolby Vision.** A failed VideoToolbox session sends the stream to FFmpeg. The software handoff transfers static colour and mastering information, while the Dolby Vision path relies on VideoToolbox attaching each frame’s RPU. FFmpeg exposes that RPU as separate frame data. If a profile 5 session is unavailable, following the software recipe sends its proprietary base picture to the layer without the metadata it needs. The Log records the visible result when that attachment is absent: green and purple. Distinguish a temporarily unavailable decoder from an unsupported stream, and specify how a software-decoded frame reaches a supported Dolby Vision presentation path with its matching metadata. Keep that fallback open until the handoff is established. [Fallback rule](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:392) · [Software handoff](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:422) · [iPad observation](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1709) · [FFmpeg 9.0.2 HEVC decoder](https://github.com/FFmpeg/FFmpeg/blob/n9.0.2/libavcodec/hevc/hevcdec.c#L3132-L3139) · [Apple decoder availability error](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/videotoolbox/kvtvideodecodernotavailablenowerr)

3. **HIGH — The 4K queue budget undercounts uncompressed pictures by a factor of two.** The plan budgets about 12 MB per 2160p 10-bit picture. Apple’s `x420` and `xf20` store each 10-bit component in 16 bits. At 3840 × 2160, one 4:2:0 picture therefore needs **24,883,200 bytes before row padding**. Sixteen such pictures require about **398 MB** before decoder, renderer or audio allocations. Admission based on the stated estimate can exceed the intended memory limit while every queue check passes. Reserve bytes using the negotiated format and aligned plane sizes, then account for the actual backing allocation. [Footprint rule](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:560) · [Probe’s `xf20` format](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1690) · [Apple’s installed iOS 27.2 pixel-format definitions](/Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer/Platforms/iPhoneOS.platform/Developer/SDKs/iPhoneOS27.2.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/CoreVideo.framework/Headers/CVPixelBuffer.h:111)

4. **MEDIUM — E-AC-3 dependent substreams advance the audio clock twice.** A-2 instructs the engine to enqueue each compressed frame separately and advance time by the sample count in its header. In a valid multichannel E-AC-3 program, an independent syncframe and its dependent syncframe carry channels for the **same** interval. Two six-block syncframes at 48 kHz can therefore describe 32 ms of sound. The prescribed count schedules 64 ms, separating channels that belong together and causing drift or repeated timing resets. Assemble the complete access unit for the selected program and advance the clock once per represented interval. [Plan line 503](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:503) · [RFC 4598, program sets](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4598.html#section-2.1.2) · [ETSI TS 102 366, Annex F.2](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_ts/102300_102399/102366/01.04.01_60/ts_102366v010401p.pdf#page=194)

5. **MEDIUM — A later video-format change has no decoder recovery path.** A-2 selects the decoder at session creation or on failure of the first pictures. An H.264 or HEVC stream can change encoding parameters at a later HLS discontinuity. If the active VideoToolbox session cannot accept the new format description, playback can lose video from that boundary onward even though a fresh session could decode it. Rebuild the description when parameter sets change, ask the session whether it accepts it, and recreate or switch decoders at a random-access point while preserving the playback clock. [Decoder selection](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:392) · [Annex B handling](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:400) · [Apple’s format-change API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/videotoolbox/vtdecompressionsessioncanacceptformatdescription%28_%3Aformatdescription%3A%29) · [HLS discontinuities](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8216.html#section-4.3.2.3)

6. **MEDIUM — Exact seeking skips the picture covering the target.** The clock restarts at the first picture whose presentation timestamp is at or after the requested time. If a variable-frame-rate picture covers seconds 10 through 15, seeking to 12 discards the correct picture and starts at 15. If it is the last picture, there may be no later picture to restart the clock. Decode from the preceding random-access point, retain the picture whose presentation interval contains the target, trim overlapping audio, and anchor the clock at the requested time. [Plan line 536](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:536) · [Apple sample timing and duration](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremedia/cmsampletiminginfo)

7. **MEDIUM — An audio timestamp gap can discard valid sound before the gap.** A-2 flushes the renderer as soon as the decoder encounters a sufficiently distant timestamp. Suppose the playhead is at 10 seconds, valid audio is queued through 12, and the next packet begins at 12.5. An immediate flush discards the two seconds the listener has yet to hear, adding them to the intended half-second gap. Preserve queued audio before the discontinuity and apply the new timing at its presentation boundary. Apple also provides a time-scoped flush when queued data actually needs replacing. [Plan line 493](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:493) · [Apple audio-receiver flush semantics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avsamplebufferaudiorenderer/receiver/suggestedflushreason)

8. **MEDIUM — Successful hardware decoding does not guarantee deinterlacing.** A-2 routes decoded fields through `bwdif` only on the software path. On the hardware path it asks the session to deinterlace. Apple documents the field-mode property as optional and permits a decoder to support only some modes. A session can return pictures successfully while rejecting `DeinterlaceFields`, leaving moving edges combed. Check whether the session accepts the mode and route interlaced output through the local deinterlacer when it does not. [Plan line 427](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:427) · [Apple field-mode contract](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/videotoolbox/kvtdecompressionpropertykey_fieldmode)

9. **MEDIUM — Portrait iPhone recordings can display sideways.** The plan explicitly includes Dolby Vision recordings made on iPhone. Its custom presentation path carries colour information and pixel aspect ratio, but gives the layer no track display transform. Apple movie capture can store a landscape raster with a 90-degree track matrix. FFmpeg demux followed by presentation of decoded pixel buffers loses that orientation unless the engine applies it. Carry the track matrix into layer geometry and use the resulting coordinates for crop and bitmap subtitles. [Supported recordings](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:60) · [Picture handoff](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:415) · [Layer geometry](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:551) · [Apple’s explanation of movie track orientation](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/qa/qa1744/_index.html)

10. **MEDIUM — The iPad P2 completion statement exceeds the Log.** The slice list marks P2 done on the iPad Pro, and the assumptions summary says A2 holds for converted MEL. A2 specifies **live** conversion. The Log records playback of files converted beforehand, identifies live conversion as unbuilt, and leaves profiles 8.4 and 10 and on-device seeking open. Likewise, the evidence for presenting an app-made Dolby Vision picture is a copy of an existing 10-bit profile 5 picture with decoder-produced attachments. It does not establish the final handoff of a reconstructed FEL picture with converted metadata. An implementer using the completion statement can skip the renderer decision P2 assigns to unprobed profiles. Record the result per profile and handoff, preserve P5’s full composition gate, and carry each deferred architecture decision explicitly into its owning slice. Dolby identifies a source of profile 10 material. [A2 status](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:149) · [P2 completion statement](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1061) · [Outstanding work in the Log](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1664) · [Copied-buffer experiment](/Users/michael/Dev/vela/.agents/plans/ipados-port.md:1689) · [Dolby profile 10 content](https://professionalsupport.dolby.com/s/article/Introduction-to-Dolby-Vision-Profile-10)
